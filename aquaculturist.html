04 Global Aquaculture Context

Aquaculture has become a major component of global aquatic food production and is closely connected with food security, nutrition, employment and economic development. The continued expansion of aquaculture, however, depends not simply on producing more fish, but on consistently producing healthy juveniles. Hatchery performance therefore becomes a strategic foundation for the entire production chain.

The research positions larval nutrition inside this larger global challenge. If the first feeding stage is inefficient, production losses can occur before fish reach the grow-out stage. Consequently, improving live-feed quality can have effects beyond the laboratory: it can influence seed availability, farm productivity and the sustainability of aquaculture production.

05 The Scientific Problem Behind the Research

The central problem is not simply whether milkfish larvae can consume rotifers. The deeper question is whether the nutritional quality of those rotifers can be improved through a controlled microalgal enrichment pathway and whether that improvement translates into measurable biological benefits for the larvae.

The Research Logic

Nannochloropsis sp. provides the nutritional source → Brachionus sp. acts as the live nutritional carrier → Milkfish larvae consume the enriched rotifers → larval growth and survival become the measurable biological outcomes.

The proposal identifies a shortage of integrated information connecting the entire sequence from microalgal culture through rotifer enrichment to milkfish larval performance. This integrated chain is therefore the scientific identity of the study.

06 Why Milkfish (Chanos chanos)?

Milkfish, scientifically known as Chanos chanos, is an economically important aquaculture species in tropical and subtropical regions, particularly across Southeast Asia. Its culture depends heavily on reliable hatchery production because healthy juvenile fish are required for sustainable grow-out operations.

The larval phase is particularly sensitive because larvae transition from endogenous nutrition provided by yolk reserves toward external feeding. During this period, feed particle size, digestibility, nutritional composition, prey availability and environmental conditions can strongly influence development.

07 The Larval Bottleneck — Why First Feeding Matters

Newly feeding fish larvae possess small mouth openings, limited swimming capability and an incompletely developed digestive system. This makes the first live-feed stage one of the most delicate stages of hatchery production.

The research therefore treats feed quality as a biological variable rather than merely a feeding quantity. A live prey organism must be appropriately sized, available in sufficient numbers and nutritionally capable of supporting rapid physiological development.

Small Mouth

Prey size must match larval feeding capability.

Developing Digestion

Digestibility becomes crucial during early development.

High Sensitivity

Nutrition and environmental stress can influence survival.

08 Live Feed Science in Aquaculture

Live feeds occupy a special position in larviculture because they combine physical characteristics with biological nutrition. Rotifers, Artemia, copepods and microalgae are widely used in different hatchery systems. For early feeding, rotifers are particularly valuable because of their small size, relatively slow swimming speed, rapid reproduction and suitability for mass culture.

However, the biological value of a rotifer is not fixed. Its nutritional composition can change according to the organism's own feeding history. This is why enrichment is an important concept in hatchery nutrition.

09 Why Nannochloropsis sp.?

Nannochloropsis sp. was selected because of its rapid growth, ease of cultivation, small cell size and nutritional characteristics. The research proposal highlights its protein, lipid, pigment, vitamin, antioxidant and EPA-related nutritional value.

Its role in this research is therefore more important than simply being an algal species. It becomes the starting nutritional component of the entire experimental food chain. Changes in microalgal culture can influence rotifer culture, and changes in rotifer nutrition can subsequently influence fish larvae.

10 Microalgal Culture — Building the First Biological Layer

The first experimental phase focuses on establishing and evaluating Nannochloropsis sp. under controlled laboratory conditions. The proposal specifies Guillard's F/2 medium, sterilized seawater, controlled temperature, salinity, pH, light and continuous aeration.

11 Brachionus sp. — The Biological Nutrient Carrier

Brachionus sp. is central to the study because it acts as the biological intermediary between microalgae and fish larvae. Rotifers can consume microalgae and subsequently become prey for fish larvae, creating a practical nutrient-transfer pathway.

The rotifer's small body size, rapid reproductive capacity and suitability for mass culture make it particularly useful as a first-feeding organism. Nevertheless, the research recognizes that its nutritional value depends strongly on the diet supplied during culture and enrichment.

12 Rotifer Culture and Population Performance

The second experimental phase evaluates Brachionus sp. using cultured Nannochloropsis sp. as the principal nutritional source. The objective is not merely to produce rotifers, but to evaluate whether the microalgal culture can sustain population growth and productivity.

13 Nutritional Enrichment — Improving Live Feed

Enrichment is the process through which the biochemical quality of live prey is improved before it is supplied to fish larvae. In this study, harvested Brachionus sp. will be transferred into enrichment containers and supplied with concentrated Nannochloropsis sp.

A non-enriched rotifer group is retained as a control. This comparison is scientifically important because it allows the research to separate the effect of simply feeding rotifers from the additional effect of deliberately enriching them.

Proposed Enrichment Framework

  • Harvest Brachionus sp. from production culture.
  • Gently rinse harvested rotifers.
  • Transfer rotifers into enrichment containers.
  • Add concentrated Nannochloropsis sp. at a predetermined density.
  • Maintain gentle aeration.
  • Compare enriched and non-enriched groups.
  • Analyse nutritional differences where applicable.

One of the strongest conceptual features of the research is its chain-based structure. Instead of treating algae, rotifers and fish larvae as isolated subjects, the study connects them into one biological system.

This pathway makes the research scientifically interesting because the quality of the final biological outcome may depend on what happens at every earlier stage. A change in microalgal culture can influence rotifer nutrition; altered rotifer nutrition can influence larval feeding; and larval feeding can influence growth and survival.

15 The Research Gap

The proposal identifies a specific gap: although Nannochloropsis sp. and Brachionus sp. are routinely used in aquaculture hatcheries, comparatively limited information is available on the complete sequence of nutrient transfer from cultured Nannochloropsis through enriched Brachionus to milkfish larvae under an integrated laboratory framework.

Existing research often examines individual components—microalgal culture, rotifer production, enrichment or fish larval feeding—separately. Nusrat's research attempts to connect these stages into a single experimental logic.

16 Research Questions

  1. What are the growth characteristics and biomass production of Nannochloropsis sp. under the established laboratory conditions?
  2. How effectively does cultured Nannochloropsis sp. support population growth and nutritional contents of Brachionus sp.?
  3. Does enrichment with Nannochloropsis sp. improve the effectiveness of Brachionus sp. as live food for milkfish larvae?
  4. How does feeding Nannochloropsis-enriched Brachionus affect the growth and survival of milkfish larvae compared with non-enriched Brachionus?

17 Research Objectives

The general objective is to test the suitability of Nannochloropsis sp. and Brachionus sp. as live feed for fish larvae and evaluate whether Nannochloropsis-enriched Brachionus can improve growth and survival of Chanos chanos larvae.

Objective 01

Evaluate growth performance and biomass production of Nannochloropsis sp.

Objective 02

Determine population growth and productivity of Brachionus sp. fed with Nannochloropsis.

Objective 03

Evaluate effects of enriched Brachionus on milkfish larval growth and survival.

18 Research Hypotheses

Null Hypothesis — H₀

Enrichment of Brachionus sp. with Nannochloropsis sp. has no significant effect on the growth and survival of milkfish larvae compared with non-enriched Brachionus.

Alternative Hypothesis — H₁

Enrichment of Brachionus sp. with Nannochloropsis sp. significantly improves the growth and survival of milkfish larvae compared with non-enriched Brachionus.

19 Experimental Architecture

The research is designed as a sequential three-phase experiment. Each phase generates the biological material or information required for the next phase. This creates a logical progression rather than three unrelated experiments.

PHASE I Nannochloropsis culture and growth evaluation
PHASE II Brachionus culture and nutritional enrichment
PHASE III Milkfish larval feeding experiment

The experiments are planned at the Aquaculture Laboratory, Department of Aquaculture, Faculty of Agriculture, UPM, under controlled indoor conditions. A Completely Randomized Design is proposed, with treatments randomly assigned and at least three replicates per treatment.

20 Phase I — Nannochloropsis sp. Culture

The first phase establishes a reliable microalgal culture that can supply the next stages. Sterilized seawater enriched with Guillard's F/2 medium will be used. Starter cultures will be progressively scaled up, with continuous gentle aeration and controlled illumination.

Cell density will be measured regularly using microscopic counting methods such as a haemocytometer or Sedgwick–Rafter counting chamber. A growth curve will then be constructed to understand how the culture changes over time.

21 Measuring Microalgal Growth Scientifically

The study does not simply observe whether algae grow. It proposes quantitative indicators that describe how rapidly the population increases and how efficiently biomass is generated.

Specific Growth Rate

SGR = (ln N₂ − ln N₁) / (t₂ − t₁)

Where N₁ is the initial cell density, N₂ is the final cell density and the time difference represents the cultivation interval.

Doubling Time

DT = ln 2 / SGR

Doubling time estimates how quickly the population requires to double under the given culture conditions.

Biomass Productivity

BP = (Xf − Xi) / t

Biomass productivity represents the rate at which biomass accumulates over the cultivation period.

22 Phase II — Brachionus Culture

The second phase uses the cultured Nannochloropsis sp. as the primary nutritional source for Brachionus sp. Population density, specific population growth rate and productivity will be monitored.

The purpose is to determine whether the established microalgal culture can function as a reliable biological input for rotifer production before the enrichment stage and larval feeding experiment.

Population Density

Number of rotifers measured per mL.

Population Growth Rate

Change in population size over time.

23 Nutritional Enrichment Protocol

A portion of harvested Brachionus sp. will undergo enrichment using concentrated Nannochloropsis sp. The proposal identifies a preliminary enrichment density of 10–20 × 10⁶ Nannochloropsis cells mL⁻¹ and an enrichment period of approximately 12–24 hours under gentle aeration, while noting that the final protocol requires experimental confirmation.

Scientific Comparison

The study maintains both non-enriched and enriched rotifer groups. Where appropriate, proximate composition and fatty-acid profiles can be compared to determine whether enrichment changes the nutritional composition of the live feed.

24 Phase III — Milkfish Larval Feeding Experiment

The third phase represents the final biological test. Milkfish larvae of similar age, developmental stage and size will be randomly distributed into experimental units. Before feeding trials begin, larvae will be acclimatized to laboratory conditions.

The objective is to determine whether the biological improvements generated upstream in the live-feed system are ultimately reflected in larval growth and survival.

26 Measuring Larval Growth

Growth performance will be evaluated using measurable biometric indicators. These measurements provide a quantitative basis for determining whether one feeding strategy produces better biological performance than another.

Length Gain

LG = Final Length − Initial Length

Weight Gain

WG = Final Weight − Initial Weight

Specific Growth Rate

SGR = [(ln Wf − ln Wi) / t] × 100

The combined interpretation of length, weight and specific growth rate provides a more complete picture than relying on a single growth measurement.

27 Survival Rate — The Most Direct Hatchery Outcome

Survival is one of the most important outcomes because improved larval growth has limited commercial value if mortality remains high. At the end of the experiment, surviving larvae will be counted and compared with the initial stocked population.

Survival (%) = (Final Larvae / Initial Larvae) × 100

Where possible, daily mortality records can also provide temporal information and help identify whether mortality is concentrated at particular stages of the feeding period.

28 Water Quality — Controlling the Hidden Variables

Nutrition cannot be evaluated fairly if environmental conditions differ substantially between treatments. The research therefore includes regular monitoring of temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen, salinity and ammonia.

Daily

Temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen and salinity.

Every 2–3 Days

Ammonia monitoring according to the proposed protocol.

Continuous Principle

Maintain appropriate aeration and comparable environmental conditions.

This environmental monitoring is important because differences in water quality can otherwise become confounding factors, making it difficult to determine whether observed differences are actually caused by feeding treatments.

29 Statistical Analysis — Turning Observations into Evidence

The proposal specifies that data will be expressed as mean ± standard deviation and tested for assumptions before inferential analysis. Normality is planned to be evaluated using the Shapiro–Wilk test and homogeneity of variance using Levene's test.

One-way ANOVA will then be used to examine treatment differences. Where significant differences are detected, Duncan's Multiple Range Test (DMRT) is proposed for separating treatment means at P < 0.05. Statistical analysis is planned using SPSS Version 29.

DATA Measurements from experiments
CHECK Normality & variance
ANOVA Compare treatments
DMRT Separate significant means
CONCLUSION Interpret biological evidence

32 Potential Hatchery Application

If the experimental results support the alternative hypothesis, the research could contribute to practical hatchery feeding strategies by demonstrating a more structured way of managing live-feed quality.

The practical value would not necessarily be limited to milkfish. The conceptual approach could inform future research into live-feed enrichment systems for other marine and brackish-water fish species, subject to species-specific validation.

33 Economic Relevance of the Research

Hatchery efficiency has direct economic implications. Higher larval survival and more consistent juvenile production can reduce losses occurring before fish reach grow-out systems. Reliable live-feed production may also reduce dependence on inconsistent external inputs.

The MBA component of Nusrat's academic profile becomes particularly relevant here. Scientific knowledge can identify a better biological process, while management knowledge can help evaluate production costs, operational feasibility, scalability and potential commercial adoption.

34 Environmental & Sustainability Relevance

Sustainable aquaculture increasingly requires solutions that improve production while managing resource use and environmental pressure. Microalgae-based live-feed systems can form part of broader sustainable aquaculture strategies because they connect biological production with nutritional inputs and potentially reduce inefficiencies in hatchery systems.

The research therefore sits within a wider conversation about sustainable aquaculture, food security and the development of efficient biological production systems.

35 Potential Limitations, Risks & Experimental Challenges

Like any biological experiment, this research involves variables that must be carefully controlled. Microalgal cultures can become contaminated or unstable; rotifer populations can fluctuate; larval mortality can arise from causes unrelated to feed; and environmental parameters can affect results.

Microalgal Risk

Culture contamination, unstable growth or unsuitable environmental conditions.

Rotifer Risk

Population fluctuations and inconsistent nutritional enrichment.

Larval Risk

Biological variability, stress and mortality unrelated to treatment.

Experimental Risk

Unequal environmental conditions or insufficient replication can affect interpretation.

These limitations make controlled experimental design, replication, randomization, water-quality monitoring and statistical testing essential components of the research.

36 Connection with Related Global Research

The scientific foundation of this research is supported by a broader body of literature on microalgae, rotifer enrichment and marine fish larviculture. Previous studies have examined the nutritional effects of Nannochloropsis, rotifer enrichment and live-feed quality across different fish species.

For example, research on milkfish larviculture has investigated the role of rotifers enriched with Nannochloropsis, while other studies have examined how enriched rotifers influence growth, survival and physiological development in marine fish larvae. These studies provide comparative scientific context, but they do not replace the need for species-specific validation of Nusrat's proposed integrated experimental chain.

37 Future Research Horizon

The research can provide a platform for future work beyond the immediate experiment. Once the biological relationship between microalgal enrichment, rotifer quality and milkfish larval performance is better understood, future studies could investigate optimization of enrichment duration, nutrient concentration, alternative microalgal species, biochemical profiling, larger-scale hatchery validation and cost optimization.
